# IAS 36 — Impairment of Assets

## Scope
Non-financial assets: PP&E, intangibles, goodwill, ROU assets, investments in
subsidiaries/associates/JVs. **Excludes** inventories (IAS 2), contract assets
and costs (IFRS 15), deferred tax (IAS 12), employee-benefit assets (IAS 19),
financial assets (IFRS 9 ECL), investment property/agriculture at fair value, and
non-current assets held for sale (IFRS 5).

## When to test
- **Annually (and whenever there's an indicator):** goodwill, indefinite-life
  intangibles, and intangibles not yet available for use.
- **Everything else — only when an indicator exists.** Assess at each reporting
  date. Indicators, external and internal: significant market-value decline,
  adverse changes in technology/market/economy/law, rising interest rates
  affecting value in use, carrying amount of net assets exceeding market cap,
  obsolescence/physical damage, idle assets, plans to discontinue/restructure,
  worse-than-expected economic performance.

## The test
**Recoverable amount = higher of (a) fair value less costs of disposal and (b)
value in use.** If carrying amount > recoverable amount, write down to
recoverable amount and recognize an impairment loss.
- **FVLCD** — price in an orderly transaction (IFRS 13 fair value) minus
  incremental disposal costs.
- **Value in use (VIU)** — PV of future cash flows from continuing use and
  ultimate disposal. Use pre-tax cash flows and a **pre-tax** discount rate
  reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money and asset-
  specific risks. Base cash flows on the asset's **current condition** — exclude
  future restructurings not yet committed and enhancing capex; exclude financing
  and tax cash flows.

## Cash-generating units (CGUs)
If an individual asset doesn't generate largely independent cash inflows, test
the smallest group that does — its CGU. **Goodwill** cannot be tested alone: from
acquisition, allocate it to the CGUs (or groups of CGUs) expected to benefit from
the combination's synergies — no larger than an operating segment.

## Order and allocation of an impairment loss (CGU with goodwill)
1. Reduce the carrying amount of any **goodwill** allocated to the CGU first.
2. Then allocate the remainder **pro rata** across the other assets by carrying
   amount.
3. **Floor:** don't reduce an individual asset below the highest of its own
   FVLCD, its VIU (if determinable), and zero. Redistribute any excess to other
   assets in the unit.

## Reversals — the goodwill asymmetry
Reassess at each reporting date whether a **previously recognized** impairment
(other than on goodwill) has decreased or gone. Reverse **only** if there's been
a change in the estimates used, and only up to the carrying amount that would
have existed (net of normal depreciation) had no impairment been recognized.
**Goodwill impairment is never reversed.** (This differs from US GAAP's model —
flag it if the user mixes frameworks.)

## Disclosure
For each material impairment/reversal: amount, the affected asset/CGU, events and
circumstances, and whether recoverable amount is FVLCD or VIU (with the discount
rate and key assumptions). For goodwill/indefinite-life intangibles, disclose the
allocation and the key assumptions and sensitivity in the annual test.

## Illustrative entry — CGU with goodwill
CGU carrying amount 1,000 (goodwill 200, other assets 800); recoverable amount
700 → impairment 300. Apply to goodwill first (200), then pro rata to other
assets (100).
```
Impairment loss (P&L)            300
  Goodwill                             200
  Other assets of the CGU              100
```
