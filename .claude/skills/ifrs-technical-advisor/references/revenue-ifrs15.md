# IFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers

## Scope
Applies to all contracts with customers **except** leases (IFRS 16), insurance
contracts (IFRS 17), financial instruments (IFRS 9), and certain non-monetary
exchanges between entities in the same line of business. A "customer" is a party
that contracts to obtain goods or services that are an output of the entity's
ordinary activities. Collaborations and risk-sharing arrangements may not be
customer relationships — test that first.

## The five-step model
The whole standard is one model. Apply the steps in order.

1. **Identify the contract.** A contract exists only when it has commercial
   substance, the parties have approved it and are committed, rights and payment
   terms are identifiable, and **collection is probable**. If collectibility
   fails, you're not yet in the model — consideration received is a liability
   until the criteria are met or the money is non-refundable and performance is
   done.
2. **Identify the performance obligations (POs).** A promised good/service is a
   distinct PO if the customer can benefit from it on its own or with readily
   available resources (**capable of being distinct**) *and* it is separately
   identifiable from other promises in the contract (**distinct in the context of
   the contract** — not highly interdependent/integrated). Bundle promises that
   fail either test.
3. **Determine the transaction price.** Include fixed consideration plus
   **variable consideration** (discounts, rebates, refunds, performance bonuses,
   penalties), estimated using expected value or most-likely-amount and subject
   to the **constraint** — only include variable amounts to the extent it's
   highly probable a significant reversal won't occur. Adjust for a **significant
   financing component** if the timing gap exceeds ~12 months, non-cash
   consideration (at fair value), and consideration payable to the customer
   (usually a reduction of revenue).
4. **Allocate the transaction price** to POs based on **relative standalone
   selling prices** (observable, else estimated — adjusted market, expected
   cost-plus-margin, or residual only when highly variable/uncertain). Allocate
   discounts and variable consideration to specific POs only when the allocation
   criteria are met.
5. **Recognize revenue when/as each PO is satisfied** — i.e., when control
   transfers to the customer.

## Point in time vs. over time
Revenue is recognized **over time** if any one of these is met:
- the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits as the entity
  performs (many routine services);
- the entity's performance creates/enhances an asset the customer controls as it
  is created; or
- the asset has no alternative use to the entity **and** the entity has an
  enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date.

Otherwise recognize at the **point in time** control transfers (indicators:
present right to payment, legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards,
customer acceptance). For over-time, measure progress with an output or input
method applied consistently.

## Frequent pitfalls
- **Principal vs. agent:** the party that controls the good/service before
  transfer is principal (records gross); an agent arranging for another party
  records only the net fee/commission. Test control, not just credit risk or who
  sets the price.
- **Licences:** right-to-access (IP that changes over the period → over time) vs.
  right-to-use (static IP at a point in time). Sales/usage-based royalties on
  licences of IP have a special exception — recognize as the sale/usage occurs.
- **Contract modifications:** separate contract (adds distinct goods at
  standalone price) vs. prospective vs. cumulative catch-up.
- **Contract costs:** incremental costs of obtaining a contract are
  capitalized if recoverable (practical expedient if amortization ≤ 1 year);
  costs to fulfil are capitalized only if they relate to the contract, generate
  resources, and are recoverable.
- **Warranties:** assurance-type → IAS 37 provision; service-type → separate PO.
- **Non-refundable upfront fees** usually relate to future performance, not a
  distinct PO — defer over the service/expected customer life.

## Presentation
Net contract position per contract: **contract asset** (right to consideration
conditional on something other than time) vs. **receivable** (unconditional) vs.
**contract liability** (deferred revenue). Disclose disaggregated revenue,
contract balances and movements, remaining performance obligations, and the
significant judgments made.

## Illustrative entry — deferred revenue with two POs
Software licence (point in time, SSP 800) + one-year support (over time, SSP 200),
billed 1,000 upfront on 1 Jan.
```
Cash                          1,000
  Revenue — licence                 800   (control transferred at delivery)
  Contract liability                200   (support, recognized straight-line)
```
Each month thereafter: `Contract liability 16.67 / Revenue — support 16.67`.
