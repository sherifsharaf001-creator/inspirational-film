# IAS 37 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

## Scope
Provisions (liabilities of uncertain timing or amount) and contingencies.
**Excludes** items covered by other standards: financial instruments (IFRS 9),
onerous elements of leases (IFRS 16 for the lease itself), income taxes (IAS 12),
employee benefits (IAS 19), insurance contracts (IFRS 17), and provisions arising
from contracts with customers/most executory contracts (unless onerous).

## Recognize a provision when — all three:
1. There is a **present obligation** (legal or **constructive**) as a result of a
   **past event** (an "obligating event" — the entity has no realistic
   alternative to settling);
2. an **outflow** of resources embodying economic benefits is **probable** (more
   likely than not, >50%); and
3. a **reliable estimate** can be made (almost always possible; if truly not,
   it's a contingent liability).

A **constructive obligation** arises from an established pattern of past practice,
published policies, or a specific current statement that creates a valid
expectation in others that the entity will discharge responsibilities.

## Measurement
- **Best estimate** of the expenditure to settle the obligation at the reporting
  date. For a large population, use **expected value** (probability-weighted);
  for a single obligation, the **most likely outcome** (adjusted for other likely
  outcomes).
- **Discount** to present value when the time value of money is material, using a
  **pre-tax rate** reflecting current market assessments and risks specific to
  the liability. Unwinding of the discount is a finance cost.
- Take expected disposals of assets and **reimbursements** into account only when
  virtually certain — recognize the reimbursement as a **separate asset**, capped
  at the provision amount.
- Review and adjust each reporting date; reverse if an outflow is no longer
  probable.

## Contingent liabilities vs. contingent assets
- **Contingent liability** — a possible obligation depending on uncertain future
  events, or a present obligation not recognized because outflow isn't probable or
  can't be reliably measured. **Do not recognize** — **disclose** (unless outflow
  is remote).
- **Contingent asset** — a possible asset from past events confirmed by future
  events. **Do not recognize**; disclose if an inflow is **probable**. Recognize
  only when realization is **virtually certain** (at which point it isn't
  contingent).

## Specific applications
- **Onerous contracts** — where unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations
  exceed the expected benefits, recognize a provision for the lower of the cost of
  fulfilling and any penalty for exiting. (Consider impairment of dedicated assets
  first.)
- **Restructuring** — a provision arises only when there is a **detailed formal
  plan** and a **valid expectation** has been raised (started implementation or
  announced main features to those affected). Include only **direct**
  expenditures necessarily entailed — exclude retraining/relocating continuing
  staff, marketing, and investment in new systems.
- **Future operating losses** — never provided for (no past obligating event).
- **Decommissioning/restoration** — recognize when the obligating event occurs;
  the cost is capitalized into the related asset (IAS 16) and the provision
  unwound over time (see IFRIC 1 for changes in estimates).

## Decision summary
| Likelihood of outflow | Accounting |
|-----------------------|------------|
| Probable (>50%) and estimable | **Recognize provision** |
| Possible, or not reliably estimable | **Disclose** contingent liability |
| Remote | Nothing |

## Illustrative entry — warranty provision (expected value)
Sales with warranty; expected repair cost 500,000 probability-weighted.
```
Warranty expense               500,000
  Warranty provision                 500,000
```
Actual claims are charged against the provision; remeasure the balance each
period based on updated experience.
